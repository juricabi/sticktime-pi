## What's new

- **VTX control on one pin works in the running game.** It had worked only with the game stopped: the drawing
  threads preempted the pin's bit timing (every frame went out milliseconds late, a Tramp VTX answered 4 frames
  in 1700), and switching the pin for every frame tore down and rebuilt the kernel's edge interrupt (up to 7 ms,
  while the VTX was already answering). Now the sender runs above the drawing threads, writes the pin through the
  Pi's GPIO registers directly (the interrupt stays armed, its thread raised too), and repeats a frame only when
  an edge went out more than half a bit late. The VTX is found at once and set; the page's **Line timing** shows
  the edges' lateness, the repeats, the pin's switch times and the sender's own edges seen back.
- **What one pin can't do:** while the game draws, the kernel stalls the sender now and then for a tenth of a
  millisecond (a whole bit at Tramp's 9600 baud), so about half of the exchanges with a Tramp VTX fail and are
  repeated; the VTX stays set, and it is declared lost only after ten unanswered polls. For a link that never
  misses, the user guide now recommends the two-wire UART mode (one more resistor at the Pi's header; the VTX still
  sees one wire). `docs/RESEARCH.md` has the measurements.
- The not-found message says when the Pi's own timing, not the wire, is the problem; `GET /api/vtx` has
  `stats.tx_late`; the line's log line comes at most every 30 s.
